# Drawing from the selected H3.3

The source for all three is [the selected editable signature](../06-delivery/lettering-review/H3.3/editable.svg). Its white master reproduces the selected 2600 px artwork. All three keep the same letter positions and outer bounds. The big M, the high t, the outside edges of the two lozenges and the three DSP capitals come directly from H3.3.

| Earlier decision | What it did to the word | This round |
|---|---|---|
| Narrower n bodies and shorter advances | The adjacent letters moved closer and the broad rhythm of H3.3 contracted. | Cut a little deeper into the n shoulder. Let the descending stroke turn at the original distance from the entry. |
| Shorter, lifted r beak | The hook receded into the line and lost some of its eccentricity. | Keep its forward projection. Compare the round, undercut and pen-cut terminals at the same x position. |
| Larger diamond openings and slimmer exits | The diamond and the following n met more lightly. | Keep the original facets. B and C let the join dip slightly before taking up the next entry. C also offsets the turn inside each opening. |
| Changes to the M exit and t crossbar | The first two high strokes changed emphasis. | Use the selected M and t contours intact. |
| Redrawn D and S joins, or a gap after e | The suffix read as a separate set of capitals, especially in R03. | Use the original connected suffix. C varies the sweep of the e leading into it. |

**A** retains the original outline of the n shoulder on top. The cut underneath it rises a little sooner. The underside of the r hook turns a little earlier. At 100 px the changes are difficult to distinguish from H3.3.

**B** gives the n a slightly forward crown and lets the returning foot sit a little lower. The r curls over a deeper pocket. The following diamond catches the pen as it climbs. I would compare this drawing with the original first at display size.

**C** makes the space inside the n more asymmetric and gives the r a two-sided pen terminal. On the first drawing its lower point was too insistent beside the diamond. The final has a short curved turn at the tip. The e keeps its connection to D.
