Two engines. One personal kit.

Voices 1–3 and 4–6 form two groups of low, body and top percussion. Choose a model for each voice, layer complementary sounds and balance all six in one panel.

02 / MODULATE

Give every hit a different edge.

Use an LFO for repeating motion, an Envelope for a shaped change, or Knob Wizard for evolving parameter variation. Multi-target routing lets a modulator reach different voices with independent, bipolar depths.

Open any detail at full resolution. On mobile, swipe to inspect the controls.

03 / MODELS

Choose the character. Then shape it.

The selector groups models into four families. Generalist models respond to the voice’s low, body or top position; dedicated percussion models bring their own controls. Classic Hat adds a dedicated hi-hat voice.

04 / PERFORM

Your sequencer. Your mixer. Your sound.

MX2 provides the voices; your DAW or external MIDI sequencer provides the pattern. Play it from pads, program a groove or send generated MIDI — then mix each part on its own terms.

Play the six voices

Trigger the voices with their assigned MIDI notes and adjust velocity response to suit the performance. Keep rhythm editing in the sequencer you already know.

Separate the mix

A stereo Main output and six dedicated stereo voice outputs let you EQ the kick, compress the snare and send percussion to different effects. Enable the extra outputs in a host that supports multi-output instruments.

Make room for your effects

Keep the source dry and direct, then build the space around it with your own delays, reverbs or outboard. Output availability and routing depend on the host.

Two ways to take it further

Kick + bass: use Voices 1 and 4 as complementary low voices. Reduce the pitch sweep of one, then adjust tuning, decay and balance to separate the impact from the bass body. The Jak2 preset is a starting point for this approach.

Hardware mixing: where your audio interface provides enough outputs, send the dedicated voice channels from the DAW to separate mixer inputs. Your EQ, sends and outboard become part of the instrument.

07 / ORIGIN

From Martello to MX2.

The idea began around 2014, through an interest in Eurorack, resonators and the character of struck objects. Martello grew out of that search for percussion that could be shaped at the source.

MX2 carries that approach into two engines and six voices: direct controls, model-specific parameters and room for your own sequencing and mixing choices.

The instrument has evolved; the intention remains the same. Give musicians a starting point they can tune, push and turn into their own sound.

02 / SHAPE

Motion is part of the sound.

A circuit-derived Classic IR3109 four-pole low-pass filter shapes the voice. ENV and LFO directly control the native filter depths; the 12-slot matrix opens up independent routes for animation and expression.

Follow it with the optional DSP-board effects: 16 programs plus Off, from distortion and comb filtering to delays, looper and pitch.

03 / SEQUENCE

From a note to a living pattern.

Program a bassline, play an arpeggio, or build a stored sequence. Sixteen steps, swing, gate and rhythmic division put the groove in your hands. Run freely or follow the host clock; keep notes, events, velocity and controller values within reach.

Inside the sequencer
  • Step / Arp / Sequence. A fixed pattern, an arpeggiator, or the stored sequence — the step editor stays available in all three.
  • Length and start. Shorten the cycle to any length from 1 to 16 steps, then rotate the start offset so the pattern shifts against the bar.
  • Groove. Six groove templates — Swing, Shuffle, Push, Lag, Human, Monkey — with a swing amount on top.
  • Gate and division. Gate from 5% to 100%, and rhythmic division from 1/1 down to 1/64 including triplets.
  • Per-step detail. Note, event state (rest / note / tie), velocity, and a controller value that feeds the sequencer modulation sources in every mode.
  • Clock. Free at 40–240 BPM, or host sync so the pattern follows the DAW transport.
04 / GENERATE

Unexpected ideas. Intentional control.

Change the notes and keep the rhythm. Vary the velocity, introduce rests and ties, or move the controller lane. Five selective generators let you choose what changes. LOCK holds the pattern and Seq/Arp settings while you audition another preset.

  • NOTESPitch only. Keep events and dynamics.
  • VELOCITYDynamics only. Keep the line.
  • EVENTNote, rest and tie states.
  • VALUEThe controller lane, independently.
  • SEQRegenerate the step data together.
Generation and note range
  • Random notes — change pitches only. Events, velocities and controller values are preserved.
  • Random velocity — change velocity only. Pitches, events and controller values are preserved.
  • Random event — change note, rest and tie structure only. Pitches, velocities and values are preserved.
  • Random value — change controller values only, independently of pitch.
  • Random sequence — new notes, velocities, rests and ties, and controller values. Playback settings are preserved.
  • Type 1 generation — pentatonic notes from step 1 across a range of −48 to +48 semitones. The mode keeps it constrained rather than arbitrary.
  • Lock — hold sequencer settings and the pattern across preset changes, so auditioning sounds never costs you the line.

Linux

Built for Linux and open-source software

At MontroneDSP, we are strong believers in Linux and open-source software.

Linux has become an increasingly capable platform for music production, offering excellent performance, flexibility, and a growing ecosystem of professional audio tools. We actively support Linux and test our plugins on a selection of commonly used distributions and desktop environments.

Ubuntu LTS

A stable and widely supported choice for audio production. Ubuntu LTS is our primary reference environment and is a good option for users who want a conventional Linux desktop with long-term support.

SteamOS — tested alternative

A stable, polished option for users who prefer a preconfigured KDE-based environment. Best suited to Flatpak-based DAWs such as Bitwig and user-installed plug-ins. Because SteamOS uses an immutable system image, advanced system-level audio modifications may require additional setup.

Caution: the SteamOS installer can wipe existing disk data. Follow the correct install procedure carefully, and always back up important files first.

Linux recommendations and installation

Our recommendation

If you are new to Linux and want the most straightforward, conventional setup for music production, Ubuntu LTS remains an excellent starting point.

For a preconfigured KDE desktop and Flatpak-based DAWs, SteamOS is a tested alternative — with the caveats noted above, including the installer disk-wipe risk.

Linux audio setups can vary considerably between distributions, audio servers, DAWs, drivers, and system configurations. For this reason, we recommend keeping your system updated and using the current version of your DAW and plugin host whenever possible.

Plugin formats

Our Linux plugins are distributed in VST3 format.

When installing manually, make sure your DAW is configured to scan the directory containing the plugin. After installation, restarting or rescanning your DAW may be required before the plugin appears.

System

A substantial real-time engine.

Minimum is a 64-bit processor, 4 GB of RAM and 100 MB of free disk, with a VST3-capable host. For comfortable real-time use we recommend a modern multicore CPU — Intel Core i7 or AMD Ryzen 5 or better — and 8 GB of RAM. Very small audio buffers at high sample rates cost real CPU. Host sample rates from 8 kHz to 384 kHz are supported. Filter processing quality is selectable from the right-click menu as High, Normal or Eco; Eco trades oversampling for CPU without changing the circuit.

LayaPreview

Rhythms that evolve, mutate, and stay playable.

Lo-fi drums and a performance sequencer built for ideas that move. All Laya builds will be free.

Laya brings together a curated collection of selected, experimental and previously unreleased drum-sequencing algorithms in a single instrument. Its sequencing engines range from focused techno and drum & bass generators to more exploratory rhythmic systems, with an emphasis on patterns that feel playable, mutable and alive rather than simply random.

Interface

Two ways to work, one instrument.

Laya can be operated through a traditional GUI, a fast command-line interface, or both at the same time. Generate, mutate, lock, randomize and reshape rhythms in real time, then use its internal drum voices or send the resulting MIDI directly to external instruments and drum racks.

Core

Repetition and variation, by design.

At its core, Laya is a powerful performance sequencer: deterministic when needed, unpredictable when invited, and designed to move continuously between repetition and variation.

Contribute

Join Laya Development

Have your own idea for a sequencer?

We’re open to integrating new rule-based generative sequencers into Laya. If you have an original sequencing concept, algorithm, probability system, pattern generator, or performance logic you think belongs in the project, write us an email and tell us about it.

We are specifically interested in deterministic, algorithmic, stochastic, and rule-based generative systems that can run efficiently as part of the instrument.

We do not accept generative-AI, inference-based, neural-network, cloud-compute, or GPU-dependent workflows.

If your idea can be expressed as musical rules, mathematics, state machines, probability, pattern transformation, or other lightweight algorithmic logic, we’d be happy to hear from you.
